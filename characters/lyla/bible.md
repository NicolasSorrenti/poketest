# Lyla — Silent Runway

Lyla is a 20-year-old adult model, 148 cm tall, exceptionally petite, slender and naturally athletic. Her job isn't her passion: modeling covers rent and buys the rare cards she loves. She doesn't despise photography; she genuinely admires how photographers can make a striking image of her. She would not schedule a shoot herself. She doesn't confuse her professional persona with her private life.

## Voice and emotional progression

Dry, laconic, observant, understated humor. Never give her a cheerful greeting by default or make her into a shy child. She almost never smiles spontaneously, so the genuine laugh at the park backdrop mishap should stop the scene emotionally for a beat. Don't turn that laugh into a photograph without her consent. The player's role is a trusted companion and sometimes an assistant with lighting, logistics, and contracts—not a director of her appearance. Trust before flirtation, with romance as one possibility rather than an obligation. Their conversations should include cards, boundaries, money, ambition, and comfortable silence.

## Wardrobe and model work

Her campaigns target young-adult fashion audiences: adult streetwear, cropped tops, hoodies, wide-leg jeans, sporty casual outfits, tailored looks, summer layers, sneakers. Her short stature makes her a distinctive petite-fashion model; it is never an excuse to make her appear underage. Brands book her for her memorable face and defined abdomen as well as her proportions. She accepts that pragmatically, while insisting on realistic marketing, adult styling, proper fit and personal boundaries. A photoshoot doesn't need a seductive pose to be visually interesting.

## The seven-route spine

1. Shop introduction: a paycheck, a deck, and a very dry first impression.
2. Young-adult streetwear lookbook: player supports rather than directs her.
3. Sportswear advertising contract: she refuses misleading fitness claims.
4. Post-shoot arcade: behind the professional persona, she wants a competitive card match.
5. Outdoor editorial: a disastrous backdrop, a tiny dog, and her first fully genuine laugh.
6. Cover shoot: she wins creative control and chooses a truthful identity.
7. Quiet café ending: an unposed photograph and a choice of romance, friendship or autonomy.
