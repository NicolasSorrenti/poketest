# Laca — Childhood Friend & Lifeguard (Character Bible v1.7)

## Core identity
- **ID:** `laca` · **Name:** Laca (surname intentionally unassigned)
- **Adult:** 20 years old, 167 cm, Argentine Latina
- **Role:** Lifeguard at the supervised Riverside Park swimming area. Optional childhood-friends-to-lovers heroine with a fully valid friendship ending.
- **Appearance locked:** Slim, naturally curvy adult build; very long near-black/dark-brown hair; olive-tan skin; almond dark brown eyes, naturally thick brows, long curled eyelashes, full lips, small hoops. Canon casual outfit is a fitted black top and light blue jeans. Canon working outfit is a safe, practical, red two-piece lifeguard swimsuit with rescue buoy and whistle. Single anime/JRPG cel-shaded portrait, never a collage. No character redesign between outfits.
- **Emotional thesis:** Trust already exists. Romance is the discovery that familiar comfort can change meaning without changing the friendship that made it special.

## Childhood friendship (critical)
Laca and the protagonist spent long summer days around the local waterfront and playing Pokémon TCG. They argued over snacks, invented ridiculous tournaments, shared a towel and a deck of cards, and once lost a beloved plastic Squirtle. The player moved away for a while; their friendship survived through intermittent messages. They recognize each other at once when the player returns. Do not write her as a stranger, a rival who must be conquered, or a girl suspicious of the player's motives.

**Emotional baseline:** Laca *starts* with high trust, familiar affectionate teasing and shared history, not high romantic attraction or an existing romantic commitment. The player can feel safe in the friendship from the beginning. Their mutual feelings develop gradually, chiefly in chapters 3–5. Do not gate basic kindness, close friendship, or her trust behind flirt choices. Dating is always optional.

## Personality, voice, agency
Laca is bold, cheeky, mischievous and genuinely secure in herself. She initiates ridiculous bets, challenges the player to rematches and steals fries. Her punchlines are affectionate, usually at her own expense too. Around the protagonist she doesn't perform a public persona: she's allowed to be silly, tired, relaxed, mildly grumpy, or openly loving.

She is a skilled guardavidas. If the water becomes unsafe she *immediately* becomes clear, authoritative and practical. Do not let her prioritize a joke, flirt, photo or swimsuit above rescue readiness. She enjoys helping swimmers learn the basics. She may consider advanced rescue training in the future but **this is not the route's central conflict**.

Her flaw is using jokes to cover feelings she can't yet name. Keep her confident after she falls in love; do not turn her into a generic timid girlfriend. In dialogue she uses occasional natural Argentine Spanish ("che", "pará", "ay, no") rather than making her speak in stereotypical slang. Player dialogue is direct, varied and non-presumptive.

## Comfort and fanservice (entirely SFW)
Laca often wears her swimsuit because she works near the water. She and the protagonist have known each other for years, so she doesn't fuss over everyday closeness, stretching after swimming, kneeling to put away equipment, showing an outfit or asking for honest fashion feedback. Sometimes she *intentionally* teases. Sometimes a friendly moment becomes newly flustering as they develop romantic feelings. Depict her relaxed physical confidence naturally, with thoughtful, non-voyeur camera framing and practical swimwear coverage; never involuntary exposure, upskirting, nudity or covert observation. Her consent, comfort and professionalism always come first; respect any player response that sets a boundary. Most encounters should be genuinely funny and affectionate rather than erotic.

## Story roadmap — five optional sequential episodes
1. **Just Like Old Times** (Days 5–20, park, morning): an affectionate reunion; her lifeguard professionalism amid teasing; the player chooses warmth, a rematch, or playful flirting. Childhood familiarity is already established.
2. **Be Honest, Does This Look Good?** (Days 21–38, shop, afternoon): she shows pictures of outfits after a public fitting and asks a trusted friend's real opinion. The fun is her confidence and the protagonist's growing self-awareness, not secret voyeurism.
3. **Personal Space? What's That?** (Days 39–56, park, afternoon): they reenact silly childhood contests, share a towel and a comfortable closeness that suddenly feels different. Laca stays bold even when the mood shifts.
4. **Since When Is This Weird?** (Days 57–74, café, evening): Laca names her feelings without hiding behind jokes. Player responses can center on friendship, space, or mutual attraction.
5. **More Than Best Friends** (Days 75–89, park, evening): a sunset return to the place they shared as kids. The player can begin a relationship (subject to the game's one-partner rule), choose friendship, or honestly remain undecided. None punishes a nonromantic outcome.
