# Neo Harbor — Scene-first character interactions

This replaces the permanent 8–10-command character menu with a VN-first design. Ordinary interaction should feel like a conversation, **not a daily relationship reward collection task**.

## UX rules

- Clicking a present character opens an actual exchange with at least three lines and back-and-forth. Core authored dialogue is preserved; newer characters can fall back to their pack's voice lines.
- Ordinary Talk and lighthearted teasing do **not** grant affection, trust, attraction, credits, card rewards, or game-time progress. Repeated same-day conversations cannot be farmed.
- Most chats have **no choice**. Occasional deterministic contextual invitations offer at most two natural responses (accept/explore or decline), generated by `rpg_conversations.js`, once per character/day. Outcomes, mechanics, and reward figures are never labeled on casual dialogue choices.
- **Talk / Duel / Deck** are compact controls **outside the VN dialogue**. The Deck button only appears once character-deck collaboration has been unlocked. Clicking the character starts talking automatically; the toolbar supports explicit gameplay actions.
- Existing shared activities, appointments, dates, tournaments, bond duel trials, Deck Studio, and authored story choices remain functional. The character can occasionally invite the player into them. No more always-on lists inside conversation windows.
- Authored storyline decisions continue to matter; story choices may intentionally grant relationship progress, but **not every scene needs an explicit reward**.
- After a completed dialogue line with no available response, the dialogue can be closed; no filler 'Goodbye' menu is required.

## Relationship progression / anti-grind

- An authored chapter requiring a previous route/guest/bond chapter now asks for at most 2 affection, **in addition to** the prior chapter; casual talk is not needed to unlock the next installment.
- Final romantic commitments require 3 affection and either 1 trust or 3 completed significant chapters. An exclusive commitment still prevents committing to another character. Friendship paths remain valid.
- Planned romantic outings require 3 affection and 1 trust instead of 4/2. Situational character events use 3 affection and 1 trust instead of 5/1. Optional CG metadata tiers are 0, 1, 2 and 3 affection instead of 0, 1, 3 and 5.
- Meaningful outings and actual duels may still improve relationships. There is no expectation that a player interact with **every** NPC daily to progress a chosen relationship.
- Old saves retain their earned relationship values and all already-completed stories. No retroactive subtraction or rerolling.

## Updating characters

New JSON character packs continue to define their intro, repeat, personality, story dialogue, and authored choices. Never insert universal stat-granting menu actions into their greetings. Use organically different lines for NPC voices, and reserve consequential choice prompts for important situations.

Run `npm run test:rpg`; key regression files are `tests/rpg_conversations.test.js`, `tests/rpg_story.test.js`, and `tests/rpg_world_ui.test.js`. Additional checks cover planned dates, situations, and the CG gallery.


## Narrative direction — quiet subtext, Persona 4-inspired pacing

**Never make a character say what the player can already see.** Physical activity, glances, hesitations, and changed behavior belong in `Narrator` beats, not in a speech. In a story event's `lines` array, use `{"speaker":"Narrator","text":"..."}` for short descriptions; protagonist and NPC lines keep their normal speaker IDs. The RPG visually distinguishes the narration from dialogue.

- **Description instead of self-commentary.** Bad: NPC says "I lower my head and nervously fiddle with my cards because I'm embarrassed." Better: Narrator says "The card sleeve bends between her fingers. She sets it down before it creases." NPC says "You didn't see that."
- **Evidence before explanation.** A missed train, an untouched second cup, a deck with a crossed-out list, or a player asking for one more game can communicate a change more naturally than "You helped me grow" or "I'm finally learning to trust."
- **Conversational economy.** Two short lines and one pause often convey more than a monologue. Characters interrupt, disagree, joke, dodge, or change the subject; emotional breakthroughs should be rare and character-specific.
- **Dialogue must be reactive.** Each response should relate directly to the preceding remark or visible action. Avoid isolated aphorisms, repeated reassurances and therapist-style speeches.
- **Choices must have distinct, believable responses.** Don't make every branch equally flattering or romantic. Friendship and graceful refusals should remain valid; never claim a date, victory or commitment that mechanics haven't recorded.
- **Stay inside the scene.** Use location-specific objects and stakes (bracket, deck box, ferry, receipt, café aisle); avoid imported TCG-era mechanics such as the later Supporter Trainer subtype.
- **Short screen-sized beats.** Write scene descriptions as compact, concrete moments rather than paragraphs of prose. Avoid copying characters, text or exact scenes from Persona; borrow the narrative economy, ordinary-life specificity, and social-link subtext.

Suggested review method: read the scene **without speaker names** and ask whether the NPC is still recognizable; read just the narration and see whether the physical story is understandable; then play each choice and verify that the response acknowledges it. Keep IDs, flags, chapter windows and choice kinds stable when rewriting a shipped route.

The original eight characters' revised source is `rpg_legacy_scenes.js` (32 scenes). Their metadata and mechanical outcome branches remain in `rpg_story.js`. The six guest character packs retain their original JSON schemas; any new narrated line can be authored directly there and then regenerated with `npm run characters:build`.


### Mandatory events and ambient lines

- **Season events are global.** The scheduler can play them from any location, so write their narration as a short, town-wide vignette or notice rather than asserting that the player just walked into a specific room. A town milestone may contain a character conversation, but it must not depend on a character's current schedule.
- **Bond events can be remote messages.** For `delivery: "remote_or_present"`, dialogue must make sense both face-to-face and as a short text exchange. Don't say "look at this photo", point to a chair, or describe a physical movement the player cannot witness unless the event engine branches its presentation by delivery mode.
- **Use the actual speaker.** Player-facing replies after choices should be attributed to the responding NPC, not automatically marked as Narrator. Set `speaker: "Narrator"` only for descriptive prose, environmental clues and dynamically generated season records.
- **Avoid stock therapeutic answers.** Replace "you don't have to prove yourself" and "I feel seen" with a response to something specific: a bad opening hand, a missed ferry, a table blocking an aisle, or an awkward invitation.
- **Keep short replies short.** "No.", "Seriously?", and "Five." are valid lines; quality tests should reject empty dialogue rather than enforce long sentences.
- **Don't lose writing to fallbacks.** `rpg_dialogue.js` has topic-specific exchanges and older supplemental arrays. Never allow a generic fallback array to overwrite a direct character-and-topic match. Add a regression test whenever introducing another dialogue source.
- **Preserve existing save positions.** When editing mandatory and bond events, preserve their IDs, choices and established event flags; if changing the number of lines for a scene already in a save, test how an in-progress index resumes.
- After touching `characters/*/mandatory_events.json` or `rpg_season_events.json`, regenerate and verify `rpg_character_packs.generated.js` / `rpg_season_events.js` with `npm run characters:build` and `npm run characters:check`. A generated registry is not a second source to author independently.

### Relationship finale decisions and crossovers

- **Make relationship intentions explicit.** A final romantic choice must use `kind: "commit"`; an explicit platonic choice uses `kind: "friend"`. An honest but undecided choice remains uncommitted. Do not infer a relationship from a kiss, flirtation, affectionate stat gain or supportive answer alone.
- **Do not change existing scene identity.** Keep five-part guest route IDs, choice indices, prerequisite flags, eligibility windows and existing `flag` values when upgrading relationship choice kinds; maintain save and UI compatibility.
- **Legacy saved kisses/dates:** a previously chosen index-1 romantic finale can recover a partner only when there is one qualifying candidate and no established partner or conflicting outcome. Never invent a choice between two completed routes. Add targeted old-save regressions.
- **Crossovers need all participants.** Check that both named NPCs speak with their own voices; let the protagonist contribute. Use a short `Narrator` line for actions rather than making an NPC describe a gesture.
- **Branch openings must keep their owner.** The `openingByFlag` text replaces a scene's first line. If that line is conditional NPC dialogue, the first speaker must remain that NPC, not be silently changed to `Narrator`.
- **Choices should finish the conversation.** Retain the last authored character line while showing response options. Don't insert a generic `How will you answer?` / `What would you suggest?` bubble that interrupts the scene.
- **Descriptions and responses differ.** Opening scene and activity result text belongs to `Narrator`; actual character replies belong to the character. Avoid printing focus gains, affection numbers or one-period labels inside centered VN decision options.

## Incidental callbacks from previously heard Talk (October 2026)

- Existing **Everyday**, **Character**, **Group**, and **Classic** casual Talk now receive stable `casual:<source>:<npc>:<topic>` memory identifiers. First meetings, reactive result conversations, mandatory scenes, and invitations are not treated as casual history.
- Only actually displaying the final line records a topic in `state.dialogueMemory.casualSeen`. Incomplete and unseen scenes never become prerequisites, and older save files start with no invented casual history.
- Each of the 22 everyday Talk characters has two independently written incidental asides (44 total) tied to specific prior topics. Once that prior Talk has been completed **on an earlier day**, an occasional unrelated Talk can include a natural three-line aside. If the prerequisite never happened, no new dialogue appears; the existing Talk is preserved verbatim.
- The incidental aside itself is acknowledged only when its last line is displayed (`state.dialogueMemory.asidesSeen`) and does not repeat after reload. The current topic is also remembered when the whole conversation finishes.
- Selection is pure/deterministic and intentionally occasional. Normal story and reactivity priority is unchanged. No game time, relationship points, rewards, mandatory flags, credits, or duel rules are changed. New Game+ resets the new memories with the rest of the new season.
- Regression coverage: `tests/rpg_conversations.test.js`, `tests/rpg_dialogue_memory.test.js`, and the save/reload end-to-end cases in `tests/rpg_world_ui.test.js`.
