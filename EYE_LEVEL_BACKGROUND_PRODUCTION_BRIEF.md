# Neo Campus RPG — Eye-Level Background Art Production Brief
Version: 2026-11-04
Status: Approved visual direction / production specification; assets have NOT been regenerated by this document.

## Creative direction

Create dedicated eye-level *gameplay* plates, not decorative wallpapers. The target is a stylized side-view JRPG / visual-novel hybrid: **large, expressive full-body adult NPC portraits on desktop**, natural human scale and useful swipeable room scenery on mobile. The background must look like a place where people can stand, not a picture behind a lineup.

The November 4 PC arcade screenshot is the reference for the preferred **large character presence**, neon ambience, and scenic breadth. Improve physical grounding, legibility, depth, and coexistence with the HUD. **Do not shrink the desktop portraits simply to conceal problems with the plate.** Mobile may use a lower scale or fewer visible NPCs per camera frame.

The existing top-down mode, its location maps, placement data and all campaign/duel state are strictly out of scope. Never include characters, visible UIs, logos, readable brand lettering, speech bubbles, baked-in NPC shadows or character outlines in the background image.

## Technical deliverable

- One **character-free 3200 × 1080 px** horizontal, seamless-feeling panoramic plate per location, exported as optimized sRGB PNG. No black margins, no transparent bands, no stretching, no mirrored repeats.
- Keep an original layered master or editable source when available; the game receives a flat scene PNG. Optional foreground/occluder layers can be considered *after* the first backgrounds are approved, but must not block interaction targets by default.
- The image must display credibly at both wide desktop (~16:9 browser viewport) and a narrow portrait mobile viewport. The camera will show only part of a panorama on phones and pan horizontally.
- Maintain a consistent semi-painted modern anime/JRPG finish, lighting and perspective compatible with the game's illustrated character cutouts. No extreme wide-angle lens, isometric bird's-eye view, fisheye or tilted camera.
- Use a human eye-level camera (~1.5–1.65 m), moderate 35–50 mm-equivalent framing, a straight vertical architecture and recognizable vanishing point. Horizon around the upper middle, not near the floor.
- **Avoid the giant empty top gradient** seen in the first mobile café screenshots. Show real ceiling / walls / windows, with a generous visible walkable floor that feels attached to those walls.
- Leave approximately the lower 35–45% of the image as credible visible walking/standing depth. Main sprite feet should plausibly land around 80–89% of the visible stage height, not float over tables or terminate at the border between illustration and a solid-color strip.
- Build natural, uninterrupted floor zones around the **four provisional portrait anchors** at horizontal 20%, 40%, 60%, 80% (x ≈ 640, 1280, 1920, 2560 of 3200 px). These are artwork planning anchors, **not text or markers to render**. The final renderer can choose better per-location positions after visual testing.
- The middle-bottom portion of desktop is partly occupied by the "What's next? / Travel" UI. Avoid indispensable props and high-detail visual information there; the implementation should also keep the characters' faces and feet visible above/beside this panel. Top-right contains the Eye-Level / Center buttons.
- Use depth cues: consistent light direction, contact surfaces, floor texture/perspective lines, and furniture occlusion. Keep the rear and sides detailed while reserving clear landing space for 2–5 independent NPC cutouts.
- Backgrounds must be **character-free** to prevent false duplicates of the game's NPCs. Prefer warm/cool localized lighting that also suits dynamically varied character outfits.

## Required location plates and art direction

### 1. Pixel Palace — Arcade
**Repository path:** `rpg-assets/eye-level/arcade-eye-level.png`
**Visual identity:** Neon violet, cyan, electric magenta; inviting late-evening gaming arcade. Polished reflective floor, lively cabinet lights, ceiling light strips, rows of game machines along rear and side walls, a few booths placed deeper in the room.
**Staging:** Clear continuous foreground/midground walkway; preserve four or five standing slots without placing stools, cabinets, or booths directly behind NPC feet. Vanishing point deep enough for a believable room. Cabinet tops provide atmosphere without overtaking portraits.
**Avoid:** Giant dominating arcade machines in foreground, NPC-like silhouettes baked into art, extreme reflections shaped like people, floor abruptly ending halfway down, excessive black ceiling.
**Prompt-ready direction:** Ultra-wide 3200x1080 background plate for a stylish contemporary anime JRPG arcade, human eye-level perspective, violet pink and cyan neon ceiling strips, colorful illuminated arcade cabinets lining both sides and rear, a few booths deeper into the room, polished reflective violet floor with a continuous open walkable foreground, strong consistent architectural depth, cinematic soft lighting, character-free environment, ample natural standing zones for four large full-body character portraits, high-quality hand-painted illustrated environment, no people, no text, no overlays, no logos, no UI.

## Renderer integration contract

Current renderer: `overworld/eye-level.js`
Entry page: `overworld-prototype.html`
Art directory: `rpg-assets/eye-level/`

- Keep `NeoOverworldEyeLevel` optional and preserve the top-down view verbatim.
- Restore/prefer **large portrait presentation on desktop** while tuning each location to avoid edges cutting off NPCs. Mobile should have a separate controlled size; never use one generic multiplier for all aspect ratios.
- Define a deliberate horizon/ground contact line **per background**, not only one `bottom: 12%` for every environment. Make contact shadow subtle and scene-appropriate.
- Do not fake the new plates by stretching the original portrait backgrounds or reflecting the image at the sides. Use the actual authored 3200x1080 image.
- Avoid empty colored strips above or below the visible panorama. Frame/crop the PNG while keeping vertical alignment and image proportions; re-check after browser resize/orientation change and image load.
- Character staging and camera centering must reflect HUD occlusion; center of screen is not necessarily a safe standing location on desktop.
- Do not alter RPG events, save/load, conversations, battle state, map coordinates or NPC scheduling as part of replacing the art.
- After approving each plate, tune `sceneStaging[map.id]` with that specific artwork; update the page's script cache-busting version when changing renderer behaviour.

## Production sequence and quality gate

1. **Arcade first**, matching the supplied PC example; compose with Yuna, Kaede, and Jinx-sized portraits visible, not miniature.
2. **Café second**, matching the Rest Stop mobile screenshots; eliminate the old dead upper band and improve the real standing floor.
3. **Tournament hall**, then **Park**, **Apartment**, **Library**, then **Card Shop**.
4. Check all assets in **desktop wide**, **mobile portrait**, and **mobile landscape**. Verify 1 NPC, 2 NPCs and a crowded 4–5 NPC room where applicable.
5. Approve only if heads, bodies, and feet read clearly; floor perspective aligns; mobile can pan; portraits remain tappable; large desktop silhouettes are preserved; UI does not cover important parts of characters; no sudden blank bands; and no changes occur to top-down assets.
6. Keep image decoding/performance reasonable, prefer optimized assets over huge uncompressed PNGs. Preload/swap the approved image to avoid a visible placeholder while the scene appears.

## Negative prompt / non-negotiables

No humans, NPCs, creatures, floating character shadows, baked-in character silhouettes, collage panels, anime portrait close-ups, duplicated mirrored room sections, fisheye/wide-lens distortion, impossible furniture perspective, low-resolution texture, unexplained blur, black/colored letterboxing, giant empty gradient sky/wall, stock-photo realism, copyrighted brands, readable lettering, stickers, dialogue, buttons or interface elements.
